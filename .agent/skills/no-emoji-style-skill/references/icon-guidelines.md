# Icon Guidelines

## Introduction
This document outlines the guidelines for selecting and using standard icons in place of emojis. It aims to ensure consistency, clarity, and accessibility in the use of icons across various applications and platforms.

## Design Principles
1. **Simplicity**: Icons should be simple and easily recognizable. Avoid overly complex designs that may confuse users.
2. **Consistency**: Use a consistent style for all icons within the project. This includes line thickness, color palette, and overall design language.
3. **Scalability**: Icons should be designed to be scalable without losing clarity. Ensure that they are legible at various sizes.

## Accessibility Considerations
1. **Color Contrast**: Ensure that icons have sufficient contrast against their backgrounds to be easily visible to all users, including those with visual impairments.
2. **Alternative Text**: Provide descriptive alternative text for each icon to ensure that screen readers can convey their meaning to users with disabilities.
3. **Cultural Sensitivity**: Be aware of cultural differences in icon interpretation. Choose icons that are universally understood or provide context where necessary.

## Usage Guidelines
- **Icon Selection**: Choose icons that clearly represent the intended action or concept. Refer to established icon libraries for inspiration.
- **Testing**: Test icons with real users to gather feedback on their effectiveness and clarity.
- **Documentation**: Document the purpose and usage of each icon within the project to maintain clarity for future developers.

## Conclusion
Following these guidelines will help create a more inclusive and user-friendly experience by ensuring that standard icons are used effectively in place of emojis.
